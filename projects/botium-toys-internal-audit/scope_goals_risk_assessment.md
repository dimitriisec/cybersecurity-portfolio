# Botium Toys — Scope, Goals, and Risk Assessment Report

## 1. Scope and Goals of the Audit

### **Scope**

The scope of this internal audit includes **the entire security program at Botium Toys**.  
All assets, internal processes, and procedures related to the implementation of security controls and compliance best practices must be assessed.

### **Goals**

- Review existing assets  
- Complete the controls and compliance checklist  
- Identify missing controls and compliance gaps  
- Determine which improvements are required to strengthen Botium Toys’ security posture  

---

## 2. Current Assets

The IT Department manages the following assets:

### **On‑premises equipment**
- Office hardware and systems used for daily business operations

### **Employee equipment**
- Desktops, laptops, smartphones  
- Remote workstations  
- Headsets, cables, keyboards, mice, docking stations  
- Surveillance cameras  

### **Storefront and warehouse assets**
- Retail products stored on‑site and sold online  
- Inventory stored in the adjoining warehouse  

### **Systems, software, and services**
- Accounting systems  
- Telecommunication systems  
- Databases  
- Security tools  
- E‑commerce platform  
- Inventory management systems  

### **Network and infrastructure**
- Internet access  
- Internal network  
- Data retention and storage systems  

### **Legacy systems**
- End‑of‑life systems requiring manual monitoring and maintenance  

---

## 3. Risk Assessment

### **Risk Description**

Botium Toys currently lacks adequate asset management and does not have all necessary security controls in place.  
The organization may not be fully compliant with U.S. and international regulations, increasing exposure to operational, financial, and legal risks.

---

### **Control Best Practices**

According to the **Identify** function of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF), Botium Toys must:

- Identify and classify all assets  
- Determine the impact of asset loss on business continuity  
- Establish processes for managing and protecting critical systems  

---

### **Risk Score**

**Risk Score: 8 / 10 (High)**  
This elevated score is due to missing controls, insufficient compliance practices, and unclear asset management.

---

### **Additional Comments**

The potential impact of asset loss is rated **medium**, primarily because the IT department lacks visibility into which assets are most at risk.  
However, the **risk of fines or data exposure is high** due to missing controls and incomplete compliance with regulatory requirements.

Key observations:

- All employees currently have access to internally stored data, including potential access to cardholder data and customers’ PII/SPII.  
- Encryption is not used to protect credit card information stored or processed internally.  
- Least Privilege and Separation of Duties are not implemented.  
- Availability and data integrity controls are in place.  
- A firewall is configured with appropriate security rules.  
- Antivirus software is installed and regularly monitored.  
- No Intrusion Detection System (IDS) is deployed.  
- No disaster recovery plans or data backups exist.  
- GDPR breach notification plan (72 hours) is established.  
- Privacy policies and procedures are documented and enforced.  
- Password policy exists but does not meet modern complexity standards.  
- No centralized password management system is in place.  
- Legacy systems are monitored, but without a defined schedule or clear intervention procedures.  
- Physical security is strong: locks, CCTV, and fire detection systems are functioning and up to date.
