# This scenario is based on a fictional company:

Botium Toys is a small U.S. business that designs, manufactures, and sells toys. The company operates from a single physical location, which serves simultaneously as the main office, storefront, and warehouse. In recent years, Botium Toys has expanded its online presence, attracting customers both in the United States and internationally.

As online demand grows, the company’s IT department is under increasing pressure to support global operations. The IT manager is concerned about maintaining compliance, protecting critical assets, and ensuring secure business operations as the company scales. Without a clear security strategy, the organization risks operational disruptions, data breaches, and regulatory penalties.

To address these concerns, the IT manager initiates an internal IT audit. The audit is based on the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF). The manager defines the audit scope and goals, identifies current assets, and completes a preliminary risk assessment. The purpose of the audit is to evaluate the company’s current security posture and determine which controls and compliance best practices must be implemented.

Your task is to:

- Review the IT manager’s scope, goals, and risk assessment report  
- Complete the controls and compliance checklist  
- Identify missing controls and compliance gaps  
- Provide recommendations to strengthen Botium Toys’ security posture  

This scenario simulates a real‑world internal audit process and demonstrates foundational cybersecurity skills, including risk assessment, compliance evaluation, and control analysis.
