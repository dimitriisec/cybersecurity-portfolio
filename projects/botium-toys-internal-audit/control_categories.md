# Control Categories

## 1. Overview

Security controls in cybersecurity are grouped into three main categories:

- **Administrative / Managerial controls**
- **Technical controls**
- **Physical / Operational controls**

Each category supports confidentiality, integrity, and availability by addressing different aspects of organizational security.

---

## 2. Control Categories

### **Administrative / Managerial Controls**

These controls address the human and organizational components of cybersecurity. They define how an organization manages data, assigns responsibilities, and enforces security expectations.

Examples include:

- Policies and procedures  
- Employee responsibilities  
- Access control policies  
- Account lifecycle management  
- Password policies  
- Separation of duties  

Although administrative controls are policy‑based, enforcing them often requires technical or physical controls.

---

### **Technical Controls**

Technical controls rely on hardware or software to enforce security.

Examples include:

- Firewalls  
- IDS/IPS  
- Antivirus software  
- Encryption  
- Password management systems  
- Backups  
- Manual monitoring of legacy systems  

These controls help prevent, detect, or respond to cyber threats.

---

### **Physical / Operational Controls**

These controls protect physical assets and limit unauthorized physical access.

Examples include:

- Door and cabinet locks  
- CCTV surveillance  
- Badge readers  
- Fire detection and suppression systems  
- Adequate lighting  
- Alarm system signage  

---

## 3. Control Types

Controls can also be categorized by their **purpose**:

- **Preventative** — stop an incident before it occurs  
- **Corrective** — restore systems after an incident  
- **Detective** — identify incidents in progress or after they occur  
- **Deterrent** — discourage malicious activity  

These types work together to provide **defense in depth**.
